## 1. New critical findings

### 1.1 Null results barely affect the score [reproduced]

A `neutral` outcome has `S = 0`, so it adds nothing to the numerator. Its `M` is clamped to 0.05 (`server.js:1604`), so it adds almost nothing to the denominator either (`totalWeight += |w·M|`, `:1621`). In practice, well-powered null studies are close to invisible:

| Evidence | Reported |
|---|---|
| 10 large RCTs (n=5,000 each) all finding **no effect** | 50%, certainty **Low** |
| Those 10 null RCTs **plus one n=40 cross-sectional survey** with a positive finding | **63% beneficial** |
| The n=40 survey **alone** | **99% beneficial, directional confidence "Strong"** |

This is the opposite of what evidence synthesis should do. Ten large null trials are strong evidence of no effect, and they should hold the result at the null with **High** certainty. Instead, one tiny survey tips the meter, and a single small study on its own gets the strongest label the product has.

### 1.2 The axis doesn't have one consistent meaning

`frameQuery` (`server.js:517-533`) and the extraction prompt (`:1501-1513`) give contradictory rules for null findings:

- For a **benefit** question, the left pole *is* "no effect" ("does creatine improve muscle?" → left = "No effect"). A null study should go left.
- For a **harm** question, the right pole *is* "no harm". A null study should go right.
- Both prompts also define `neutral` as "no difference found, p>0.05". So the same null study can be coded three different ways, depending on how the question was phrased and on which of the conflicting instructions the model happens to follow.

Because sampling is uncontrolled (§4.3), this ambiguity shows up as run-to-run variance. It also makes the meaning of "X% beneficial" depend on question wording. "Does coffee cause harm?" and "Is coffee beneficial?" should give consistent answers, and with these rules they can't.

**Fix:** pick one scale with a meaning that doesn't depend on phrasing, such as the *direction and size of the effect of the intervention on a named outcome*, with "harmful" and "beneficial" as two ends and **null in the middle**. Add a separate *evidence state* that says whether the evidence is precise enough to say anything (§2.2).

### 1.3 Benefits and harms are averaged onto one axis

Every outcome from every paper goes into the same scalar (`server.js:1600-1638`), and nothing restricts them to the `outcomes` the frame asked about. Take statins: fewer cardiovascular events (`supports_right`) and a higher risk of diabetes (`supports_left`). The engine averages these into one number and reports the result as **contradiction**. But the studies don't disagree. That is a **benefit–harm trade-off**, and it's exactly what a user needs to see separately. A single "% beneficial" figure can't represent it.

Multiplicity makes it worse. A paper that reports five outcomes gets five votes. **[reproduced]** One RCT reporting a benefit (1 outcome) against one RCT reporting harm (5 outcomes) gives **22%**, when it should be roughly 50%.

### 1.4 The hard filter rejects relevant papers and admits wrong ones [reproduced]

- `requiredTerms: ["vitamin d"]` lets through a paper titled *"Vitamin C and the common cold"*. The `d` subterm is under 3 characters and gets skipped (`:1093`), so only "vitamin" has to match.
- `requiredTerms: ["statin"]` (the prompt's own example, `:563`) **rejects** *"Statins and dementia risk"*. `\bstatin\b` doesn't match the plural. The filter has no handling for plurals or word forms anywhere.

### 1.5 The "broadening" fallback can only make the filter stricter

When fewer than 4 papers pass, the code retries with "requiredTerms in title only" (`server.js:2549-2571`). That set is a **strict subset** of what the first filter already accepted (required terms in the title ⊂ required-or-synonym terms in title or abstract). The fallback can never add a paper, so it's dead code that looks like a recovery path.

### 1.6 Extraction only sees the first 800 characters of each abstract

`server.js:1411` sends `p.abstract.slice(0, 800)`. A structured abstract (Background, Methods, **Results**, Conclusions) is commonly 1,500–2,500 characters, so the **Results section, which holds the effect size, CI and p-value, is often cut off**. The extractor is then guessing direction and magnitude from the Background and Methods. Of all the fixes in this review, this one probably improves accuracy the most per line changed. The relevance check has the same problem: it sees only 250 characters (`:1179`).

### 1.7 Merging duplicates throws away the best metadata

`dedupe()` (`server.js:438-452`) keeps whichever copy of a paper arrives first and discards the rest. Papers are concatenated in the order S2, then PubMed, then OpenAlex (`:2528`). So when a paper appears in both Semantic Scholar and PubMed, the **PubMed record is discarded**, and with it:

- the PubMed publication types, which are the most reliable design labels the pipeline has
- the grant list, which is the only structured funding data on the hot path

Dedupe should **merge** records field by field: PubMed for design, grants and retraction status; S2/OpenAlex for citations; any source for the abstract. Title matching should also normalise punctuation and Unicode.

### 1.8 Review articles are counted as meta-analyses

- Semantic Scholar: `publicationTypes` containing `'Review'` → `design = 'meta'` (`:609`)
- OpenAlex: any `type` containing `review` → `'meta'` (`:813`)
- PubMed: `"Clinical Trial"` → `'rct'` (`:685`). That label includes non-randomised and single-arm trials.

The pre-extraction ranking sorts by these labels (`:2584-2590`) before cutting the list to 30 papers. As a result, **narrative reviews crowd out primary trials** in the analysed set. The LLM reclassifies them afterwards, but by then the selection has already happened.

### 1.9 The divergence analysis tells the model about industry funding that doesn't exist

`analyzeDivergence` (`server.js:2374-2378`) counts papers whose abstract contains *"funded by"*, *"supported by"* or *"grant from"*. That matches **any** funding statement, including NIH and Wellcome. It also only counts papers that have *no* extraction. It then tells Claude: *"Industry funding detected in N papers"* (`:2400`). The model is fed a false premise and asked to explain the media/science gap with it.

### 1.10 Outlet credibility is effectively a constant

`domainWeight(url)` looks up the hostname. But Google News RSS links point to `news.google.com/rss/articles/…` and Bing RSS links point to `bing.com/news/apiclick…`. Neither is in `OUTLET_WEIGHTS`, so almost every article gets **weight 2**. Only Guardian items, which are hardcoded to 4, differ. As a result:

- the "credibility × recency" ranking (`:2234`) is really just recency
- the `lowQualityOutlets` divergence signal (`:2389`) is an artifact of the URL shape

Use the `<source url="…">` attribute in the Google News RSS feed (it carries the real domain) and decode Bing's `url=` parameter.

### 1.11 The interface invents links between papers and news articles

`getRelatedMedia(x)` / `getRelatedScience(x)` (`verity.html:2095-2108`) choose items whose *randomly jittered* x-position is within ±0.22–0.25 of the selected one. The results appear under **"Media coverage of this finding"** (`:2021`). Nothing connects the two. The page asserts a citation relationship that doesn't exist. Either remove this section, or match on DOI/PMID links or entity overlap, and label it honestly.

### 1.12 Other dropped or dead code paths

- `fetchCrossrefFunding` (`:935`) and `fetchNYT` (`:1942`) are **never called**. The comments describe NYT as a live source.
- The OpenAlex funding lookups run **after** the result object has been built (`:2684`). Their output never reaches the response or the cache.
- `paper-N` references for papers without a DOI or PMID never match an extraction on the server (`:2647-2652`). Those papers keep their API-derived design and get no outcomes on their cards.
- DOI and PMID matching uses `.includes()` (`:2649-2650`). PMID `1234` matches `pmid:12345`. Compare for exact equality instead.
- Papers with no extraction default to `'mixed'` and are plotted in the centre of the strip (`verity.html:2735`). A failed extraction looks the same as genuinely mixed evidence.
- `isMedicalTopic` (`:2167`, `:2275`) matches words like "outcome" and "condition", so almost every query counts as medical.
- The media percentage is **calculated by the LLM** (`:2327`) rather than counted from the stances it returns.
- Cache entries carry no algorithm version. Fixing the estimator would keep serving old, wrong answers for up to 30 days.

## 2. Making the estimator measure the right thing

AUDIT.md §6 P1 still applies. Here is a concrete design that fixes §1.1–§1.3 together.

### 2.1 Report per outcome, not one global scalar

1. Ask `frameQuery` for **at most 3 prespecified outcomes**, each tagged as `benefit` or `harm` ("critical" or "important", as in GRADE).
2. Have extraction attach each finding to **one of those outcome IDs** or to `other`. Ignore `other` when scoring.
3. Score each outcome separately with **one row per study per outcome**. If a study reports the same outcome more than once, take its primary result.
4. Present a **Summary-of-Findings table**: one row per outcome, showing direction, pooled or typical effect, number of studies, total N, and certainty. A headline number is fine as a summary, but it should never be the only output.

### 2.2 Separate "which direction" from "do we know"

For each outcome, classify the evidence into one of five states. This replaces the meaningless 50%.

| State | Rule (sketch) |
|---|---|
| `insufficient` | < 3 eligible studies, or total N below a threshold |
| `consistent_benefit` / `consistent_harm` | lower credible bound of the beneficial share is > 0.5 (or < 0.5), *and* the pooled effect isn't trivial |
| `consistent_null` | most of the weight is in precise nulls (CI excludes a minimal important difference) |
| `conflicting` | high-quality studies have significant results in *both* directions |

Show **directional agreement** with a Jeffreys Beta interval, as AUDIT.md §6 item 11 proposed. Show **effect size** separately, pooled where the numbers exist (§2.3). Suppress the meter for `insufficient`.

### 2.3 Extract numbers, check them against the text, and pool them

Extraction should return:

- `effect: {measure: "RR|OR|HR|MD|SMD|%", point, ciLow, ciHigh, n}`
- `quote`: a verbatim sentence from the abstract

**Reject any extraction whose `quote` is not a substring of the abstract.** This is a cheap, deterministic check against hallucination.

Where at least 3 studies give a compatible measure (ratio measures on the log scale, or SMD), run a random-effects meta-analysis with the **Hartung–Knapp** adjustment, because the number of studies is small. Report τ², I², the CI and the **prediction interval**. With this in place, magnitude stops cancelling out (AUDIT §1.1), and precise nulls finally carry their weight (§1.1 above).

### 2.4 Weighting that holds up to scrutiny

- **One design prior**, not `D × B` (design counted twice).
- Risk of bias as a *downgrade*, in the GRADE style, and not a multiplier. Use:
  - industry funding, sourced from PubMed grants and Crossref funders, actually wired in
  - lack of randomisation
  - small N
  - retraction or erratum
- **Overlap control.** A meta-analysis and the RCTs it already includes must not both vote. Use OpenAlex `referenced_works`: if a selected primary study is cited by a selected meta-analysis, drop the primary study or down-weight it to zero. This replaces the arrival-order independence weight (AUDIT §1.2).
- Separate design categories for **animal, in vitro and case report**. Leave them out of human consensus by default and show them in their own tier.
- Treat **unstated N** as unknown precision, not as a mild 12% penalty (`:1559`).
- **Recency.** Prefer the newest high-quality systematic review as the anchor (the "umbrella-first" approach), then add primary studies published *after* its search date.

### 2.5 Selection bias in the retrieved set

Papers are ranked for inclusion by citation count (`:2587`). Positive and surprising findings are cited more, so this **imports citation bias into the sample**, and that is exactly the bias evidence synthesis is supposed to correct. Rank by relevance plus design. Use citations only to break ties, and never as weight. Also:

- Report how many results each source returned against how many were kept, as a PRISMA-style flow. The counts already exist in `meta`.
- Add a basic **small-study / asymmetry flag** when pooling is possible: Egger's test with k ≥ 10, otherwise a note that it can't be assessed.

## 3. Retrieval and source quality

Each source returns only its top 15–25 results, so recall is low. These changes give the most improvement for the effort:

1. **Use field-aware PubMed queries.** Run two searches: one restricted to systematic reviews (`systematic[sb]`) and one restricted to randomized controlled trials (`randomized controlled trial[pt]`), then fill the remaining slots. Leaving out tags because they "silently fail if wrong" (`:571`) loses most of PubMed's precision. Validate the generated query with esearch's `querytranslation` and fall back if it errors.
2. **Exclude retractions.** Check PubMed `Retracted Publication` / `Retraction of Publication`, Crossref `update-to` / `relation`, and OpenAlex `is_retracted`. All three are free. Today this check doesn't exist anywhere in the code.
3. **Add Europe PMC.** It offers publication types, preprint flags, open-access full-text links, and often the full abstract where S2 truncates it.
4. **Add ClinicalTrials.gov results.** Registered trials with posted results but no publication give a direct signal of publication bias, and it would set Verity apart from comparable tools.
5. **Flag preprints and predatory journals.** Mark medRxiv/bioRxiv and use a DOAJ / journal-quality signal so they can be tiered separately.
6. **Keep the PubMed record's structure.** Keep the `Label=` attributes on `AbstractText` (RESULTS / CONCLUSIONS) and decode HTML entities. `stripHtml` leaves `&lt;`, `&#x2265;` and similar in the text, so values like "≥" reach the extractor as entity codes.
7. **Use one consistent date window.** Deep mode extends only PubMed to 15 years (`:2513`), while S2 and OpenAlex stay at 10. For many questions, landmark trials are older than 10 years, so let the frame choose the window.
8. **Parse the year properly.** It currently defaults to 2020 when missing (`:691`, `:821`). Use PubMed `MedlineDate` / `ArticleDate`, and store `null` when unknown.

## 4. Getting more accuracy from the models

### 4.1 Use structured outputs instead of `parseJSON`

Every call relies on "Respond ONLY with valid JSON" plus regex fence-stripping. Switch to `output_config: { format: { type: "json_schema", schema } }`, which needs an SDK upgrade from `^0.39`. Also check `stop_reason` (`max_tokens`, `refusal`) before parsing. Today a truncated or refused response becomes a silent `JSON.parse` exception.

### 4.2 Treat abstracts as untrusted input

Abstracts, headlines and the user's query are pasted straight into prompts. Wrap them in delimiters (`<paper id="…">…</paper>`), tell the model that their contents are data, and keep the instructions in `system`. Combined with structured outputs and rendering text with `textContent`, this also closes the stored-XSS path.

### 4.3 Make the same question get the same answer

Current Sonnet models don't accept `temperature`, so the AUDIT.md suggestion to set it doesn't apply. Get determinism another way:

- **Cache each paper's extraction**, keyed by `(DOI or PMID, outcome-schema hash, prompt version)`. The same paper is then coded the same way in every query that retrieves it. This is the biggest stability win, and it also cuts cost.
- **Check extraction consistency.** Run the cheap model (`claude-haiku-5-5`) twice per paper. When direction or design disagrees, escalate to the reasoning model with adaptive thinking. Store the agreement rate and expose it as a quality signal.
- Add the algorithm version and the prompt version to the result cache key.

### 4.4 Assign models by task

| Task | Now | Suggested |
|---|---|---|
| Relevance screen, media filter, media stance | Haiku 4.5 / Sonnet 5 | `claude-haiku-5-5` (about 10× cheaper than Haiku 4.5) |
| Per-paper extraction | Sonnet 5, batches of 12 | Haiku 5.5 first, escalating to Sonnet 5.5 on disagreement. One paper per request gives clean failure isolation, and the static instructions can be prompt-cached. |
| Frame, synthesis, verdict | Sonnet 5, thinking disabled | `claude-sonnet-5-5` or `claude-opus-5-5`. Note: on these models `thinking: {type:'disabled'}` returns a **400**. Lower `effort` instead. |
| Incremental worker (once it's real) | n/a | Message Batches API (50% cheaper, not latency-sensitive) |

Before any model switch, check the parameter changes in the model migration guide. The current `NO_THINKING` constant will break on Sonnet 5.5 and Opus 5.5.

### 4.5 Prompt caching

The extraction instructions (about 1.5k tokens) are repeated in every batch, and the frame prompt (about 1.2k tokens) is static. Move both into `system` with `cache_control`. This is free and cuts input cost on cache hits.

### 4.6 Ground the synthesis in specific papers

`synthesize` receives titles and the top 6 "drivers", and is told "NEVER invent numbers". Nothing enforces that. Instead:

- give it numbered evidence rows (with `quote`, effect and CI)
- require an `[n]` marker after every claim
- check after generation that every number in the prose appears in a cited row
- render citations as links to the evidence table

Return structured paragraphs, not HTML.

### 4.7 Remove the advice tone from the verdict

`generateVerdict` tells the model to be *"like a trusted doctor friend"* and to say *"what should a reasonable person DO"*. For a tool that synthesises evidence, the verdict should describe the evidence ("Across N trials, X reduced Y by …; certainty moderate"), not recommend an action, and it should come with a visible not-medical-advice notice.

## 5. Presenting sources and answers

1. **Evidence table as the main view.** Columns: design, N, population, effect with CI, the verbatim quote, year, funding, risk-of-bias flags. Make it sortable and filterable (design tier, human/animal, date). Group it by outcome.
2. **Forest plot** for each outcome with a pooled estimate. Otherwise show a "direction plot" where each **position comes from the computed effect or contribution** (`scoring.contributions`), never from `Math.random()`.
3. **Show why each paper counts:** its weight breakdown, and why it was included or excluded (hard filter, relevance screen, overlap removal). This makes the claim "every number traces to a real paper" true, which it isn't today.
4. **Reproducibility block.** Show the exact search strings per database, the date of the search, the result counts at each stage, the algorithm and prompt version, and a permalink to a frozen snapshot.
5. **Show the real abstract.** Remove the hardcoded `getAbstract()` / `mediaFraming` maps and render `p.abstract` and `m.framing`, which are already in the payload.
6. **Uncertainty first.** Show the evidence state and certainty *before* the percentage. For `insufficient`, show no meter at all.
7. **Media section.** Classify articles against the outcome-level conclusion, not the scalar. Label it as framing in headlines, and drop the made-up "coverage of this finding" links (§1.11).

## 6. Engineering robustness

These fixes are smaller but they matter. Security items are in AUDIT.md §3, and all of them are still open.

- **There are no tests and no CI.** The scoring engine is pure functions, so it's easy to test. The cases in §1.1–§1.4 should be the first regression tests, together with a small fixture set of real abstracts paired with expected extractions.
- **`server.js` is a single 2,844-line file.** Split it into `sources/`, `filter/`, `extract/`, `score/`, `media/`, and `routes/`. The `hardFilter` matching logic is copied verbatim **four times** (`:1087`, `:1113`, `:1135`, `:2555`), so extract it into one function.
- **Log volume.** The funding debug blocks write several lines per paper on every search. They should go behind a `DEBUG` flag.
- **`validateRelevanceWithClaude` runs batches serially** (`:1176`). Run them in parallel like the extraction batches.
- **External API hygiene.** There's no retry or backoff for S2 429s. The S2 call has no API key, and S2's unauthenticated pool is shared and heavily throttled. Each search sends **about 25 outbound news requests** (5 queries × 3 sources + 10 `site:` queries), which risks Google blocking the server.
- **Report what failed.** Add `meta.extractionFailures`, `meta.sourceErrors`, and `meta.papersWithoutExtraction`, and show the user a warning when coverage is partial.
- **Request lifecycle.** There's no overall timeout, and nothing aborts the work when the client disconnects. Add an `AbortController` per request, and add SSE progress events to replace the fake timed checklist.
- **SDK version.** `@anthropic-ai/sdk ^0.39` is old. Upgrading it is a prerequisite for structured outputs and the newer model parameters.

## 7. Validation: how to show the platform is scientifically trustworthy

Until this exists, nobody can tell whether a change makes Verity more accurate or less. This matters more than any single fix above.

1. **Gold set.** Take about 150 questions with adjudicated answers from Cochrane review conclusions, USPSTF and WHO guidance. Record direction per outcome, GRADE certainty, and the pooled effect where one exists. Include known nulls, known harms, benefit–harm trade-offs, and recently reversed findings.
2. **Metrics:**
   - direction accuracy per outcome
   - agreement between the evidence state and the gold certainty (weighted κ)
   - interval coverage of the pooled effect
   - **stability**: the same question run 5 times, and 3 paraphrases of the question; report the spread
   - extraction field accuracy against a small hand-coded sample of abstracts
3. **Run it on every change to prompts, models or scoring.** Publish the results on the methodology page. A product that claims existing sources are "a little wrong" needs to publish its own error bars.

### Appendix: how the reproduced numbers were produced

The code in `server.js:457-467` (`DESIGN_PRIOR`), `:1073-1104` (`hardFilter`) and `:1541-1686` (`biasWeight`, `precisionWeight`, `directionScore`, `computeConsensus`) was copied unchanged into a standalone script. It was then run with these synthetic extractions:

- `populationMatch` and `interventionMatch` = 1, `precision` = 0.8
- `magnitude` = 0.6 for directional outcomes and 0.05 for neutral ones
- sample sizes as stated in each table

Results:

| Scenario | Output |
|---|---|
| 10 × RCT n=5000 neutral | rightPct 50, certainty Low, "Inconclusive" |
| + 1 × cross-sectional n=40 `supports_right` | rightPct 63 |
| 1 × cross-sectional n=40 `supports_right` alone | rightPct 99, certainty Very low, directional "Strong" |
| 1 × RCT (1 outcome, right) vs 1 × RCT (5 outcomes, left) | rightPct 22 |
| `hardFilter(["vitamin d"])` on "Vitamin C and the common cold" | passes |
| `hardFilter(["statin"])` on "Statins and dementia risk" | rejected |
